MedVault Iraq App Design

The new insurance screens for Iraqi members, built on the app's current design: the blue-to-white background, Poppins, grey borderless inputs, navy buttons, turquoise action tiles and turquoise active tab. Buttons link to the next screen, so you can click through each flow.

All names, numbers and amounts are sample data. [Insurer name] is a placeholder until the insurer's branding is supplied.

1 · First launch and welcome

The app works out the country by itself while the splash is showing, then shows that country's welcome slides. Nobody picks a country. The country picker appears only when the signals can't decide.

How the country is decidedChecked in this order. The first clear answer wins.
  1. Returning userA signed-in account keeps its own country, wherever the phone is.
  2. Network locationOur server looks up the request's IP address. No permission prompt.
  3. Phone signalsSIM or network country on Android, and the device region on iOS.
  4. AskSignals disagree, or point to neither country (a VPN, or travel abroad): show the picker, 1.8.

GPS permission is asked only later, when the member opens Find care. A location prompt at launch would show a system alert before the first screen. Prompting at launch is the same mistake that got build 11.0.0 rejected at review (see CLAUDE.md, App startup order).

2 · Sign-up and insurance linking

The existing sign-up and OTP, then the new insurance step. Linking never blocks sign-up: a failed check or an unreachable insurer still lets the member in.

3 · My insurance

The Iraq home replaces the labs rail with the member's cover. The four turquoise tiles reuse the lab-detail action tiles. Every balance shows when it was read from the insurer, and the card opens from the header, as the VMC card does today.

4 · Renewing the policy

Renewal opens 30 days before the policy ends. Members with an individual or family policy renew in the app: they pick a plan, the co-pay and who's covered, then pay. An employer policy is renewed by HR, so its members get a status screen with no actions. Taking payment in the app is Phase 3 and needs the insurer to confirm it can collect premiums this way.

5 · Family, medications and results

Every family member has their own card, results and reminders. Family is reached from Account › Family members and from the + on the card screen. Medications and results live in a new Health tab, filtered by person. Results and reminders work for everyone, insured or not, and in both countries. In Lebanon, results that arrive from a lab appear here automatically.

6 · Wearables and doctor reports

MedVault reads what the member's watch already records through the phone's health store: Apple Health on iPhone (Apple Watch and apps that save to it) and Health Connect on Android (Samsung Galaxy Watch, Fitbit, Pixel Watch and others). It never talks to a watch directly. Readings are shown and shared with a doctor. MedVault doesn't diagnose, and the insurer never sees any of it.

7 · Ask MedVault, the AI assistant

A floating button on Home, Find care and the other main screens opens a chat about the member's own cover, claims, results and medicines. It's hidden on screens that already have a main action button, so the two never compete. It answers from the member's data through our server, shows where each answer came from, and offers the next step as a button. It explains; it doesn't diagnose. Like the AI Summary today, it can be switched off per insurer, and the insurer never reads the chats.

8 · Find care and claims

The provider directory reuses the labs list and lab detail patterns, including the search bar, the circular logos and the turquoise action tiles. Claims follow the status-tinted cards from Test Results. Sending a claim takes two steps, and the documents asked for come from the insurer's settings for that claim type.

9 · Account and notifications

Account keeps today's grouped rows and adds an Insurance group. The country is shown but locked, because changing it changes which services the account has.

10 · Arabic, right to left

The same screens in Arabic. The whole layout mirrors: navigation, back arrows, progress bars and lists. Amounts and card numbers keep Western digits; whether to use Arabic-Indic digits is a question for the insurer. Plan and benefit names come from the insurer's settings in each language.
