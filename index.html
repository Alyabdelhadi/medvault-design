MedVault App Design

One app, the same screens for every member wherever they are, restructured to the Developer Brief of 12 October 2026: five tabs, Vault · Results · Insurance · Health · More, in that order, with every feature the brief lists placed in its tab and mapped to a phase in section 11. Built on the app's current design: the blue-to-white background, Poppins, grey borderless inputs, navy buttons, turquoise tiles and turquoise active tab. Buttons link to the next screen, so each flow can be clicked through.

One app, no country versionsEvery member gets the same tabs and the same screens. Nothing is hidden or swapped by country.
  1. Same view for everyoneVault, Results, Insurance, Health and More look identical wherever the member is. Language (English or Arabic) is the only choice, and it is the member's.
  2. Not live yet: Coming soonA feature that isn't available to this member keeps its place on the screen, greyed, with a Coming soon tag.
  3. Tap it: Coming soon screenIt explains what is coming and offers Notify me. Never an error, never an empty tab. See 3.4 and 5.2.
  4. Why a feature isn't liveThe member's insurer or lab isn't connected yet, or the feature ships in a later phase (badges on this board).

1 · First launch and welcome

The same welcome for everyone, everywhere: no country detection and no country picker. The slides walk through the four things MedVault does: results, the insurance card, claims and privacy. Language follows the device and can be changed on the first slide.

2 · Sign-up and insurance linking

The existing sign-up and OTP, then the new insurance step. Linking never blocks sign-up: a failed check or an unreachable insurer still lets the member in.

3 · Vault

The main dashboard and the first of the five tabs. The brief asks for four things, and they appear in its order: the latest results with links to the full reports, in-app advertising banners, the insurance summary (card, coverage status and quick links into Insurance), and upcoming reminders for medications, tests and appointments. Every block is a shortcut into another tab; nothing is managed here.

4 · Results

Today's Test Results screen, kept as it is: the same chips, "Whose results?", month groups, order cards, PDF download, AI summary and capability-aware gauge. Two additions from the brief: the lab's logo next to each result, and a Test reminders section for upcoming and recurring tests. Results arrive automatically from every connected lab. A member whose lab isn't connected yet adds results themselves, and the same list shows both.

5 · Insurance

Everything the brief lists for insurance, under one tab: the digital card with eligibility and active coverage, the policy, the Table of Benefits with limits, exclusions and co-pays, the covered provider network, claims, pre-approvals and payments. Balances always show when they were read from the insurer.

6 · Renewing the policy

Part of the Insurance tab. Renewal opens 30 days before the policy ends. Members with an individual or family policy renew in the app: they pick a plan, the co-pay and who is covered, then pay through the same payment sheet as everything else. An employer policy is renewed by HR, so its members get a status screen with no actions.

7 · Health

The brief's four Health sections, as tabs across the top: Sensors, Health figures, Medications and Prescriptions. Readings come through the phone's health store: Apple Health on iPhone and Health Connect on Android. Every figure names its source. MedVault shows and shares readings; it doesn't diagnose, and the insurer never sees any of it.

8 · More

Secondary features: profile, family members, settings, language, support and account management. Every family member has their own card, results and reminders, and every tab can be filtered by person.

9 · Ask MedVault, the AI assistant

Phase 3
The brief places AI-supported assistance in Phase 3. A floating button on Vault and the provider network opens a chat about the member's own cover, claims, results and medicines. It answers from the member's data through our server, shows where each answer came from, and offers the next step as a button. It explains; it doesn't diagnose. It can be switched off per insurer, and the insurer never reads the chats.

10 · Arabic, right to left

Every screen in English and Arabic, chosen by the member on the first welcome slide or in More, with full right-to-left layout: navigation, back arrows, progress bars and lists all mirror. Amounts and card numbers keep Western digits; whether to use Arabic-Indic digits is a question for the insurer. Plan and benefit names come from the insurer's settings in each language.

11 · Phases and dependencies

The brief's request: every tab feature mapped to a delivery phase, with what it waits on. Until a feature is live for a member, its place in the app shows Coming soon. A screen without a badge on this board is Phase 1. Where a feature is split, the first part ships in the earlier phase and the badge marks the later part. The phase split is a proposal for review, not something the brief fixes.

Phase 1Digital health foundationOne app for every member, English and Arabic with full RTL. Al Ettihad and iCare connected. Self-service eligibility, benefits and network. Self-pay onboarding, booking, payments, banners.
Phase 2Healthcare network expansionHospitals, labs, radiology and pharmacies integrated. Claims and pre-approvals streamlined. More providers in the network.
Phase 3Intelligent health servicesAI assistance and analytics, telehealth, caregiver tools, medication fulfilment and delivery.
